One Piece Wallet in 3D

A structural sketch, not a scan. The photographs show a spine wrapping three inner leaves and two tight loops, but not the flat cut pattern. This folds one 1 mm strip with that structure, widened to the wallet's width. It has no pockets, flap or silver cross, panels can pass through each other, and nothing here is a leather simulation.

What it shows

  • One strip: bottom panel, spine, top panel, loop, leaf A, left fold, leaf B, loop, leaf C. That gives three inner leaves and two loops at one end, as in the edge-on photographs.
  • Each fold turns through a fixed length of sheet, so a fold that opens gets a larger radius.
  • The layers sit one loop-gap apart, so the loop radius also sets the stack the spine wraps.

What the colours are

  • The closed-form strain on the outside of each fold, from the simulation's geometry, scaled with the fold angle (the pleat result: strain follows the angle).
  • Not a leather simulation. It does not include set, the thread ties or the grain.

What would make it real

  • The flat pattern, or photographs of the wallet unfolded flat.
  • A measured closed thickness and each loop's radius.
  • The report now derives the spine from the loops in the same way: 7 mm at the default radius, and 5 to 19 mm across the range it allows for the loops.

Source: retailer text and product photographs in the Wallet-corpus- archive (item map01). The photographs are research documentation only and are not shown here. Sizes S 11.5 x 7.5, M 13 x 9, L 15 x 10 cm are read as the closed footprint. Strain is the closed form t / (2R + t), where t is the sheet thickness and R the inside radius.
